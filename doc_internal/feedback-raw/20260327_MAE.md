

# Feedback: Arrival Resources Web App

**Organization:** Mutual Aid Eastie  
**Date:** 2026-03-27  
**Participants:** Two core members  

## Overall Response

The conversation focused on two main areas: how Mutual Aid Eastie is represented through service tags, and what kinds of neighborhood resources should be included in the broader Arrival Resources database. The discussion was useful not only for correcting information about this organization, but also for raising larger questions about how the project defines services, categories, and the scope of "arrival resources."

## Main Feedback from Participants

1. **Revise how Mutual Aid Eastie is described through service tags**  
   The participants agreed with most of the tags currently associated with Mutual Aid Eastie, but they raised an important concern about the label **"mental health."** They said they would prefer something like **"emotional healing"** instead, because their activities in this area include things like Reiki and yoga, rather than formal psychological or clinical mental health services. They also felt that the term "mental health" carries negative stereotypes that do not reflect how they want this work to be understood.

2. **Add or better reflect some of Mutual Aid Eastie's actual activities**  
   Participants noted that the current tags or descriptions may not fully capture the kinds of support they provide. They mentioned, for example:
   - helping people get licensed to do child care
   - helping people obtain a driver's license
   - organizing a gardening circle in the summer in front of the East Boston Public Library

3. **Suggest additional neighborhood resources to include**  
   Participants suggested several organizations or places that may be worth adding to the database, including:
   - City Life / Vida Urbana
   - the Resource Center in East Boston at 282 Meridian Street
   - Central Presente
   - GreenRoots (Chelsea)
   - Mario Umana Academy in East Boston
   - Eastie Farm

## My Own Reflections Following the Discussion

1. **Standardized tags may not always align with how organizations describe themselves**  
   This conversation highlighted a tension between creating a standardized tagging system and respecting the language that organizations themselves use to describe their work. In some cases, existing tags may be too broad, too generic, or not well aligned with community preferences.

2. **Publicly available information has limits**  
   Much of the current database is based on what organizations publicly present on their websites. That means there are limits to how accurately the app can capture what is actually happening on the ground, especially when organizations offer forms of support that are informal, seasonal, relational, or not prominently described online.

3. **Need to think more carefully about what should count as a service tag**  
   Some activities mentioned in the meeting—such as help obtaining a driver's license—seem very important in practice, especially because mobility can be crucial for everyday life. But it is not yet clear whether these should become standalone service tags, or whether doing so would require broader verification across many organizations.

4. **Need to clarify the scope of “arrival resources”**  
   The suggestions raised a broader question about what kinds of organizations should be included in the app. Some neighborhood resources are important and widely used by newcomers, even if they are not specifically targeted at newcomers. This includes examples such as schools, health centers, libraries, community gardens, and other local institutions. The question is whether including more of these neighborhood resources strengthens the project, or risks diluting its focus.

5. **Need to think about future update and governance models**  
   This discussion also prompted reflection on whether organizations should eventually have a more direct way to suggest or edit how they are described. The current feedback form exists, but all feedback so far has come through in-person conversations rather than through the form. This raises questions about whether a more efficient mechanism is needed, and about who will be responsible for ongoing revisions and maintenance.

## Action Items / Specific Notes

- Consider replacing the **"mental health"** tag for Mutual Aid Eastie with language that better reflects how the organization describes this work, such as **"emotional healing."**
- Review whether the current description of Mutual Aid Eastie should better reflect support for child care licensing, driver's licenses, and seasonal gardening activities.
- Review the suggested organizations / places for possible inclusion in the database.

## Key Takeaways

- Feedback on a single organization can reveal larger issues in the tagging and categorization system.
- Standardized service tags may not always match the language or self-understanding of community organizations.
- The conversation raised important questions about what kinds of neighborhood institutions should count as “arrival resources.”
- The project may eventually need a clearer strategy for updates, corrections, and shared stewardship of organizational descriptions.
